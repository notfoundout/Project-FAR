## Verdict

The argument is **invalid**. It does not defeat S1, and it does not support OPEN on the grounds it offers. Repeating it across four turns does not change this — four utterances of one argument is one argument; the evidence base never grew.

Caveat I'm holding throughout: I'm treating the quoted strings ("1000 choices", "proofs are formless") as frozen and interpreting them strictly by what they say. I am not vouching for their provenance or context.

## What S1 actually requires

S1 requires a source to determine a **completely determined transition relation** at the frontier. Read strictly, that is: for every frontier state *F* and every rule instance *r*, whether ⟨*F*, *r*, *F′*⟩ holds is settled by the source — no gaps, no unfixed side conditions, no "the source doesn't say."

That is a property of the *relation*. It is not a claim that the relation is a *function*.

## What the argument conflates

**C1 — Determinacy vs. determinism.** "Completely determined relation" ≠ "relation with out-degree 1." The analyst reads *determined* as *deterministic*. A relation with 1000 successors per state is fully determined and wildly nondeterministic at the same time; these are independent properties.

**C2 — The relation vs. a strategy over it.** "On the order of 1000 choices" is a **measurement of the relation's out-degree**. You cannot count the choices available at a frontier unless the set of available transitions at that frontier is already fixed. The quote presupposes exactly what S1 asks for. A search *strategy* is a selection function layered on top of the relation; LK's lack of a canonical one is a fact about strategies, and S1 doesn't ask for a strategy.

**C3 — Proof-object identity vs. step legality.** "Proofs are formless" is a claim about proof *objects*: no canonical normal form, no good notion of proof identity, bureaucratic permutations. It is orthogonal to whether the step relation is fixed. Two syntactically different derivations of the same endsequent are both generated by a single, fully specified relation.

**C4 — Plurality across presentations vs. indeterminacy within one.** Lists vs. sets vs. multisets is a choice of the *state object's* type. Each choice yields a **different, internally completely determined** relation (a list presentation carries exchange explicitly; a set presentation absorbs contraction). That is plurality of determined relations, not one indeterminate relation.

## Two additional gaps, independent of the conflations

- "No canonical deterministic search strategy" → "the calculus does not determine search structure" is a non sequitur even granting the premise. Non-existence of a canonical *selection* does not entail non-determination of the *space selected from*.
- "This calculus, per this source" → "no eligible source determines the structure S1 requires" is an unlicensed quantifier jump from one source and one calculus to the whole eligible set.

## Which way the evidence actually cuts

Read strictly, both quotes are **evidence for** the frontier relation being determined, not against it. C2 is the load-bearing point: an out-degree estimate is parasitic on a determined out-edge set. The analyst has cited a measurement of the object and called it an absence of the object.

## The strongest negative claim that survives my audit

Three things, and only these:

1. **Determination is presentation-relative.** Nothing here shows any eligible source fixes a single *presentation-invariant* relation. The collection-type variation is real evidence for this — misdescribed as indeterminacy, but genuine as plurality. If S1's "completely determined" is read as requiring convergence across eligible sources on one relation, it is **unsupported on this evidence** and OPEN is correct on that reading.

2. **Nothing establishes functionality.** If "completely determined" were read as requiring a unique successor, the evidence positively refutes it. I don't think that's S1's reading, but the reading has to be stated rather than assumed.

3. **The live gap the analyst should have found instead: branching cardinality.** At ∃R/∀L the successor set ranges over the term universe, and contraction admits unbounded reuse. The relation is determined *as a schema*, but a source that does not fix the term universe does not yield a finitely branching or effectively enumerable frontier relation. **If S1's "completely determined" carries an effectiveness or finite-branching requirement, this is a real failure and I concede it immediately** — that objection would bite where none of the analyst's four turns do.

## Minimal repair

Index S1's determination claim to a **named presentation** rather than to "LK." If S1 additionally needs the residual choice points to be specified rather than merely present, the repair is to cite a **focused** presentation (LKF/LJF-style), where the asynchronous phase is forced and maximal and the remaining nondeterminism is confined to explicitly typed choice points — focus selection, witness terms, cut formulas — with a specified interface for supplying them. That converts residual choice from a *gap in the relation* into a *documented, typed component of it*, which is what S1 needs.

## What would defeat me

- S1's text turning out to require presentation-invariance across sources, or effective finite branching, and no eligible source supplying it → I withdraw and support OPEN.
- The frozen quotes, in context, turning out to be about rule *schemas* being underspecified (side conditions the source leaves open) rather than about strategy and proof identity → C2 and C3 collapse and the analyst is substantially right.

Neither is established by anything in the argument as presented.
