## 1. Design intent — not delivered

B1 holds the **task** fixed and varies only the **wording**. It keeps: the same object (two descriptions of one episode), the same criterion (sameness in every respect that matters), and the same nine axes, in the same order and with the same content. It varies: sentence construction, register, and the framing sentence.

B1 deliberately does **not** vary the axis decomposition. That is B2's job. Conflating the two is what invalidated the superseded Packet B.

B1 introduces **no** new substantive criterion. In particular it does not mention misleading a reader, usefulness, or downstream decision quality — the superseded Packet B did, which silently changed the question rather than its wording.

## 2. The question

Some systems take up a question or a problem and end at a conclusion, a judgement, or a decision. Take one occasion on which that happened, and take two separate written accounts of that same occasion.

What has to be common to the two accounts before they count as accounts of the same occasion in every way that counts?

---

## 3. Set out separately

Give each of the following. Where you cannot settle one, say so and say why, rather than filling it in.

- **A.** Which systems, or kinds of system, your account is about. The scope is yours to set; assume none is intended.
- **B.** Which observable facts or behaviours count.
- **C.** Which differences may be let go without loss.
- **D.** What being "common to" the two accounts amounts to.
- **E.** What "the same in every way that counts" amounts to.
- **F.** Which ways of turning one account into the other are legitimate.
- **G.** What your account takes for granted.
- **H.** Whether rival answers to B–F can be set against one another in any natural way, and what would make it natural.
- **I.** Or whether no single answer to B–F can be defended.

Note any counterexamples you built, approaches you abandoned, and anything left unresolved.

### Shapes an answer may take

An answer of any of these shapes is complete and acceptable. **The list is neither exhaustive nor ranked, and its order carries no preference.** A shape not listed is equally acceptable.

- One set of requirements holds across every system you admit.
- One set holds, given assumptions you state.
- Several rival sets are equally defensible.
- Different kinds of system need different sets.
- No non-trivial set exists; every candidate is empty or arbitrary.
- The question will not settle as put, or you cannot reach a stable answer.

**There is no target answer.** Do not try to work one out from the wording, and do not aim at agreement with any body of work you suspect lies behind it.
