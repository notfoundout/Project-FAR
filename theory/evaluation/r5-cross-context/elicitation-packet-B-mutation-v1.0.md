# Elicitation Packet B — mutation control

**FROZEN. Do not run now.** This packet exists so that prompt-sensitivity can be measured later. It asks the same underlying question as Packet A without preserving its surface structure.

Same delivery rules as Packet A: §2 and §3 verbatim, nothing else attached, no clarification of intent, no disclosure of the commissioning body of work.

---

## 1. Design intent, retained for the record and not delivered

Packet A frames the question as *what must carry over between two descriptions of one episode*. Packet B frames it as *when two records may be treated as interchangeable for a downstream user*. The two framings differ in:

- **direction** — A asks what is retained; B asks what a substitution must not disturb;
- **primitive object** — A takes the episode as given and compares descriptions; B takes the records as given and asks about substitutability;
- **structure** — A supplies lettered sub-questions; B supplies a single open request with a checklist appended;
- **stance** — A is descriptive; B is operational.

Nothing in B is a paraphrase of A's sub-questions in the same order. If a future reader judges B to be a surface rewrite of A, B has failed as a mutation control and must be replaced before use.

---

## 2. The question

Someone keeps records of occasions on which a system worked through a problem and reached an outcome. They are considering replacing one record with a differently written record of the same occasion.

Under what conditions may that substitution be made without misleading anyone who later relies on the record? Justify your conditions.

---

## 3. Please also state

In whatever order suits your answer:

- what kinds of system, if any, your conditions are meant to cover, and which you exclude;
- what a later reader could legitimately want from such a record;
- what may safely differ between the two records;
- what may not differ;
- how you would decide whether two records are interchangeable in a disputed case;
- what would have to be true of a rewriting procedure for you to trust it;
- what your answer assumes;
- whether competing sets of conditions can be ranked against one another, and on what basis;
- or whether no single set of conditions can be justified.

Record counterexamples, rejected approaches, unresolved points, and every source you used, marking each source as primary/original, authoritative technical, or secondary/expository.

Any shape of answer is acceptable, and the following are equally so, in no particular order and not exhaustively: that one set of conditions covers every case you admit; that one set covers them under assumptions you state; that several sets are equally good; that different kinds of case need different sets; that no non-trivial set exists; or that the request cannot be settled as asked or you cannot reach a stable answer. There is no target answer.

---

## 4. Interpretation rule, frozen before execution

How A/B agreement and disagreement will be read is fixed now so that it cannot be chosen after seeing results.

| Observation | Permitted reading | Prohibited reading |
|---|---|---|
| A and B yield materially the same conditions | Weak evidence that the elicited answer is not an artefact of one surface form. | **Not** evidence that the answer is natural, correct, canonical, or independent of framing generally. Two framings are two points, not a sample of the framing space. |
| A and B yield materially different conditions | Evidence that the answer is framing-sensitive. This is a **positive finding about the question**, and is recorded as such. | **Not** automatic refutation of either answer, and **not** evidence that the underlying question is unanswerable. |
| One packet yields a stable answer and the other does not | Evidence about elicitability under that framing. | **Not** evidence that the stable answer is the better one. |

Prompt sensitivity is itself evidence and is reported as a first-class result rather than as noise to be averaged away.

**Minimum design.** One A run and one B run with the same respondent are **not** independent and may only be reported as a within-respondent framing check. Cross-respondent A/B requires two respondents at the same or higher evidence tier, and the tier of the pair is the lower of the two.
