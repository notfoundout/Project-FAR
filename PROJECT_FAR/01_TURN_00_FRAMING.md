## §2 — Project FAR's exact objective (lines 14–48)

```
PROJECT FAR'S EXACT OBJECTIVE

Project FAR is investigating whether there exists a broad, independently justified class of structured investigations/reasoning systems for which a small, nontrivial, representation-independent functional architecture emerges.

The current candidate decomposition is approximately:

* Construct
* Differentiate
* Restrict
* Resolve

Call this candidate RCCD for convenience.

RCCD is not assumed true.

The stronger research objective is:

Identify the broadest independently justified class of structured investigations that produces nontrivial invariant operations without defining the class, representation, mappings, or admissibility conditions so that RCCD—or any preferred result—is inserted into the premises.

The result may be:

* RCCD;
* RCCD plus additional primitive operation(s);
* fewer than four primitives;
* a different basis entirely;
* multiple incomparable minimal bases;
* no finite basis;
* invariants that are not best characterized as operations;
* a restricted-domain theorem rather than a universal one;
* or failure of the foundational hypothesis.

Any of these is acceptable.

Discovery outranks preservation of FAR.
```

```
What is the maximally broad independently justified class of structured investigations on which nontrivial representation-independent structural invariants exist at all?

[
\max_D ; \text{Breadth}(D)

\operatorname{Inv}(D) \neq \varnothing
```

```
CRITICAL ANTI-CIRCULARITY RULES

Reject any proposed FAR result if it depends materially on any of the following:

1. defining "structured investigation" in terms equivalent to the candidate operations;
2. selecting target systems because they visibly instantiate RCCD;
3. defining adapters using RCCD categories;
4. interpreting arbitrary transformations as RCCD after the fact;
5. using translation existence alone as evidence of deep common structure;
6. treating representability as equivalence;
7. treating expressive equivalence as operational equivalence;
8. treating one convenient decomposition as a minimal decomposition;
9. treating absence of known counterexamples as proof;
10. treating broadness obtained through vacuous encoding as genuine universality.

Pay special attention to pathological encoding results.

Very permissive translation notions can make radically different systems "translatable" without establishing meaningful shared structure.

Therefore require stronger criteria where appropriate:

* compositionality;
* naturality;
* preservation;
* reflection;
* operational correspondence;
* bounded information loss;
* equivalence invariance;
* non-artificiality of encoding;
* minimality;
* universal characterization;
* independent motivation.
```
